    'Puke' seems pretty strong to me. Could you analyse it a bit further?

    The message is sound: intolerance and bigotry do contribute to wars and conflicts; children learn intolerance their parents; therefore anything that helps to break the cycle.

    I think you've done an excellent job of explaining it's pukeworthiness James.

    More generally, I don't have a problem with what the kids are saying as a piece of propaganda. And propaganda is important to keep things going.

    But the West's traditions of tolerance were hard won - through a couple of centuries of mayhem and bloodshed following the reformation. Scripting a few kids play the role that Rousseau marked out for them and lecture us all on tolerance - well let me just say I don't like it. Lets also say that there's a deep irony in messages like this which presuppose some kind of integrity of the kids on account of their age when in fact they're just mouthing slogans they've been taught.

    The issues they are speaking of matter too much to be made light of. I expect most of the kids are pretty good on racism (at least in theory) having had it drummed into them. What are they like on bullying? Sexuality? Popularity? Celebrity? The things that are close to them? I expect they're in no position to lecture.

    Apart from which, my own instant response to propaganda is to assert its opposite. What's so wrong with intolerance? Intolerance to violence, simplistic moralism, political correctness etc.

    That's a serious point by the way and related to the point about struggle. 'Tolerance' as a virtue like any virtue (like courage, selflessness, inclusiveness or whatever) unaccompanied by some sense of its cost, its context etc is empty at best, but delivered in a high handed moralistic tone comes perilously close to its opposite as I tried to explain here.

    The problem with "tolerance" is that it is a negative virtue and a relatively modern construct (you don't hear say Aristotle expounding the virtues of "tolerance", people penning odes to "tolerance" through the ages or singing songs about longing to be "tolerated" etc.).

    It is also incoherent: We shall tolerate all except the intolerant.

    Which is why some critics will say that it actually leads to negative outcomes such as self-righteousness, inequality

    Walzer, from On Toleration:

    In ordinary speech, it is often said that toleration is a relationship of inequality where the tolerated groups or individuals are cast in an inferior position. To tolerate someone else is an act of power, to be tolerated is an acceptance of weakness

    Better, I think to seek positive virtues such as generosity, hospitality, justice, right-living...
