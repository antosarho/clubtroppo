Probing the nadir of punditry

Posted on August 17, 2013 by Ken Parish
Posted in Politics - national, Journalism

Troppo readers who have followed my meanderings about asylum seeker policy over the years will realise that I have some fairly basic differences with the Greens on that issue ((although not on the fundamental fact that many if not most of them need our compassion and support – it's a matter of how best to deliver  it and that's where I think the Greens are both naive and self-indulgent. ~ KP)).

However, I am utterly repulsed by Tory media pundits like Andrew Bolt and Ben Fordham who advance the proposition that Labor and the Greens should be blamed for the one thousand or more asylum seekers who have drowned at sea over the last few years trying to reach Australia. An interview by Fordham yesterday with Greens Senator Sarah Hanson-Young is a particularly odious example of the genre:

Tension mounted after Mr Fordham asked if Ms Hanson-Young took responsibility for the deaths that have occurred at sea since the Rudd Government dismantled John Howard's immigration policies. …

"You supported a policy which has led to 50,000 people arriving here ever since and 1000 plus people drowning to the bottom of the ocean and dying," Mr Fordham said. "They're not on the earth anymore."

In reality, asylum seekers have full agency in their own decisions.   They are making difficult choices in situations that are sometimes close to impossible. If Australian laws are effectively changed in the ways that Rudd and Abbott are promising, all that will happen is that most asylum seekers will turn their attentions to other first world country targets that now look like a better bet. Moreover, irregular entry to many of those countries is also dangerous. Lots of asylum seekers drown while trying to get from North Africa to Italy or Spain, even more while trying to get from Cuba or South America to the United States, and some even suffocate while being smuggled overland into Europe in shipping containers. Government authorities are not responsible  per se for asylum seeker deaths any more than police are responsible for the actions of murderers and rapists just because they don't stop all of them.

  9. Ken Parish

    You certainly have a point Chris, and I'm anything but a supporter of the Greens' policies on asylum seekers. I was actually thinking more of the fact that right wing pundits like F Fordham and Andrew Bolt level the same accusation of responsibility for deaths at sea at L Labor politicians like Rudd and Gillard. However, their situation is that they abandoned the Pacific Solution (offshore processing) at a time when it seemed to them and many in the community including many in the Liberal party that it was unnecessary surplus repression because boat people had stopped coming. Abandoning the Pacific Solution was not self-evidently stupid at the time. Moreover, as soon as it became apparent from the fact that boat arrivals soon began to increase in the wake of abandonment of the Howard policy, Rudd/Gillard started to readopt tougher policies. One can argue that they did so too slowly but you can't argue that they failed to respond positively and responsibly to the situation as it emerged.

    The Greens are in a quite different situation. They continue to condemn all of the existing tough measures. However, their position is it least internally not inconsistent with a responsible approach. They argue (along with Julian Burnside, Frank Brennan and others) that the appropriate approach is large scale and rapid offshore processing in Indonesia and Malaysia, with people who qualify immediately getting Australian protection visas. Of course, that policy if adopted would certainly "stop the boats", because there would be no need to jump on one. People could be rapidly processed in Indonesia and then jump on a plane without taking any risks. Thus, at least in that sense it is unfair to accuse the Greens of complicity in deaths at sea, because their policies if adopted would avoid those deaths. However, what their policy blithely ignores is that such a policy would immediately result in a very high proportion of the tens of millions of asylum seekers worldwide flocking to Indonesia to be processed and get a fast track to get into Australia. It is an idiotic and completely unworkable policy position, and one that could only be advocated by a party that knows it will never be in government.
